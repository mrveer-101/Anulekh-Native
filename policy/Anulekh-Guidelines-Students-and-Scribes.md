# **Anulekh — Guidelines for Students & Scribes** 

_A scribe for every voice.  ·  Draft v0.1 · A practical handbook · Not legal advice_ 

This handbook is the plain-language companion to the Anulekh Terms & Conditions. The Terms set out the rules you agree to; these Guidelines show you how to live by them in practice — before a match, on examination day, and afterwards. Where a detail here and the Terms ever seem to differ, the Terms and the examination authority's own rules take precedence. 

#### **The six non-negotiables** 

1.  A scribe writes only what is dictated — no prompting, hinting, explaining, or correcting. 

2.  No money changes hands. Ever. 

3.  Every user is identity-verified before a match. 

4.  Minors are protected first — safeguarding beats convenience every time. 

5.  Personal and disability data is confidential. 

6.  The examination authority has the final word. 

## **Part A — How Anulekh works, step by step** 

**1.** Register. A student (or their guardian, if the student is a minor) and a scribe each create an account and provide the required details. 

**2.** Verify. Both sides complete identity verification. A scribe cannot be matched until this is done. 

**3.** Match. Anulekh connects a student with an eligible, verified scribe, surfacing the authority's eligibility requirements for both to confirm. 

**4.** Prepare. Student and scribe agree the practical details — exam date, centre, timing, and how the student prefers to communicate — through the platform. 

**5.** Examination day. The scribe writes exactly what the student dictates, within the structure the authority provides. 

**6.** Afterwards. Confidentiality continues. Either party may report a concern; both may give feedback. 

## **Part B — For students** 

### **Before you request a scribe** 

- Check what your examination authority requires — a disability certificate, UDID card, or a request form — and have it ready. 

- Know your entitlements: under the RPwD Act you may be eligible for a scribe and for compensatory time (commonly 20 minutes per hour). 

- Think about how you like to work: your reading pace, whether you want answers read back, and any communication preferences. You can share these with your scribe. 

### **On examination day** 

- **You lead.** Dictate at a pace that suits you. Ask for a read-back whenever you want one. 

- **Your scribe writes, not thinks.** If a scribe offers hints, explanations, or “help” with an answer, say no and report it afterwards — it can disqualify you.

- **Corrections are yours to make.** A scribe changes a word only when you dictate the change. 

### **Your rights** 

- A fair, faithful transcription of exactly what you say.

- A scribe who never asks you for money, gifts, or favours. 

- A safe, respectful interaction — with a guardian involved if you are a minor. 

## **Part C — For scribes** 

### **Before you accept a match** 

- Complete your identity verification — you cannot be matched until you do. 

- Confirm you meet the authority's eligibility rules (often a lower qualification or grade than the candidate, and not a teacher of the subject). 

- Be sure you can honour the commitment. A no-show can cost a student their exam. 

### **The golden rule, in practice** 

#### **When in doubt, do less** 

Read the question exactly as written. Write the answer exactly as spoken. Read it back when asked. Do not explain, hint, judge, or “improve” anything. If you're unsure whether something crosses the line, it does — so don't. 

A few situations scribes ask about: 

- **The student pauses or seems stuck.** Wait quietly. You may not prompt or suggest. 

- **You spot an obvious spelling or factual error.** Write it as dictated. You correct only what the student dictates as a correction. 

- **The student asks “is this right?”** You cannot say. Gently remind them you can only write and read back. 

- **The question is ambiguous.** Read it again as written. You may not interpret or paraphrase it. 

### **On examination day** 

- Arrive on time and follow every instruction of the examination authority and invigilators. 

- Carry only what the authority permits — no notes, phones, or unpermitted devices. 

- Write legibly and at the student's pace; offer read-backs when asked. 

### **Afterwards** 

- Keep everything you saw confidential — permanently. 

- Report anything that concerned you, especially about a student's safety. 

## **Part D — Communicating well, together** 

- **Stay on the platform.** Keep your conversation within Anulekh's channels. It keeps everyone accountable and lets a guardian be looped in when the student is a minor. 

- **Accessibility first.** Scribes follow the student's stated preferences for how they want to communicate and be assisted.

- **Respect always.** No harassment, no discrimination on any ground, no personal agendas. The examination is the only purpose of the interaction. 

- **Reliability.** If plans change, tell the other side as early as you can. 

## **Part E — Safeguarding, especially with minors** 

Many students are minors, and scribes may be adults who are, at first, strangers. These practices are not optional — they are how Anulekh keeps young people safe. 

- **Do** keep every conversation on the platform, involve the guardian, and meet only in connection with the examination. 

- **Do** report immediately, through Anulekh, anything that makes a minor feel unsafe. Safety reports are handled first. 

- **Don't** ask a minor to move off-platform, share personal contact details, or meet socially. 

- **Don't** ask a minor to keep any secret from their guardian or from Anulekh. 

- **Don't** seek a minor's address, social media, photos, or personal details a match doesn't need. 

#### **Zero tolerance** 

Any conduct that sexualises, grooms, isolates, or otherwise endangers a minor leads to immediate, permanent removal and, where warranted, referral to law enforcement. If you see it, report it. 

## **Part F — The money line** 

It is simple: no money, no gifts, no favours, in either direction, ever. Anulekh does not charge students, and scribes are volunteers. If anyone asks you for payment or a favour — or offers one — do not go along with it, and report it. This single rule is what protects students from exploitation and keeps Anulekh distinct from a paid-scribe marketplace. 

## **Part G — Reporting a concern** 

Anyone can raise a concern — misconduct, a safety worry, a policy breach, or a privacy issue — through the platform's reporting channel. What to expect: 

- Reports about a minor's safety, or about examination integrity, are treated as urgent and reviewed immediately. 

- Anulekh acknowledges reports, investigates fairly, and keeps the reporter's identity confidential where possible. 

- Where required, Anulekh escalates to the appropriate authorities.
